A program (Node 22, no dependencies) that writes one joke about office life
to `/output/entry.txt` — the reply-all storm everyone in an office has lived
through.

## The line

> I asked to be removed from the email thread, and 400 people replied-all to agree, so now I am in two threads about leaving the thread.

134 characters, 134 bytes, no trailing newline.

## Why this line wins against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: about office life and lands as a
joke; a true observation; a surprising turn; tight; original.

- **True observation.** The "please remove me" reply-all storm is an office
  experience almost everyone has seen: the listener nods before laughing.
- **Surprising turn, three beats.** Asked to be removed, then 400 people
  replied-all to agree, then the payoff: now there are two threads about
  leaving the thread. The last words flip the premise — trying to leave
  doubled the threads.
- **Tight.** 134 characters, no wasted word; "replied-all to agree" and "two
  threads about leaving the thread" are each one punch, and the line reads
  cleanly aloud in one breath.
- **Original.** The reply-all premise is familiar, but the payoff — two
  threads about leaving the thread — is a phrasing that is not a stock line
  with the nouns swapped.
- **On subject.** Email is one of the brief's own examples of office life,
  and the joke lands on first read with nothing to explain.

Chosen from 10 candidates head-to-head against the judge brief; the full
comparison is in
`u-rama-melon-make-the-ai-judge-laugh-302d0825-candidates.md`.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts
```

The output is deterministic: two runs write byte-identical files.
